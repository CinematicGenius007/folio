i. a shelf, not a service

Your bookmarks already live in your browser. Folio just gives them a field to run around in, every page you kept, found in a keystroke, and nothing ever leaves the meadow.

{{summary}}

ii. what it does

Small things, done gently.

  1. 01

    Your browser’s own bookmarks

    No new account and no copy kept elsewhere. Folio reads and writes the bookmark tree you already have, so your browser’s sync keeps working as it always did. Uninstall it and every bookmark is still there.

  2. 02

    Found in a keystroke

    Fuzzy search over titles, links, tags, notes and folder paths at once. Results arrive while you type, even with thousands of pages kept.

  3. 03

    Folders inside folders

    Nest as deep as you like. A breadcrumb shows where you are, a switcher finds any folder by part of its name, and a tree picker moves bookmarks.

  4. 04

    Tags and notes, kept close

    Add a few words to a link and find it by them later. They stay in your browser profile, with a one-file backup whenever you want it.

  5. 05

    Nothing is lost by accident

    Deleted bookmarks wait 30 days and come back with Z, tags and notes included. Saving a page twice shows you where it already lives.

  6. 06

    Kept on this machine

    No server, no analytics, no telemetry. On Chromium browsers Folio makes no network requests at all.

iii. three ways in

Wherever your hands already are.

The popup
Press Alt S on any page, pick a folder with a number key, and it is saved. Type / to find a folder at any depth.
The full page
Your whole library in one calm column, with search, folders, tags and every shortcut. Alt Shift F opens it.
The side panel
The same library beside your tabs, in Chromium’s side panel or Firefox’s sidebar, with Alt Shift B.

iv. the whole map

Eight keys, and you’re anywhere.

  • j kwalk the list (J and K)
  • ↵open it (Enter)
  • /search anything (slash)
  • cswitch folder (C)
  • eedit (E)
  • mmove elsewhere (M)
  • ztake it back (Z)
  • ?every other key (question mark)

The rest, and how to change them, are in the keyboard guide.

v. how it keeps quiet

One loop, one direction.

Folio’s pages never ask the browser for your bookmarks directly. They read a small local index, kept in your browser’s own storage, which is why search feels instant.

When you save, move or delete, the change goes straight to the browser’s bookmarks. A background script notices, and it alone updates the index. Every open Folio window stays in step, and the browser always remains the source of truth.

That is all there is. No sync service, no account, nothing to trust but your browser. Read how it works.

vi. let it in

Three steps, then it’s yours.

  1. one. Download the release for Chromium browsers, or for Firefox and Zen, from GitHub. Or build it in a minute.
  2. two. Load it from your browser’s extensions page. The install guide walks through every browser.
  3. three. Press ? and wander.

What it asks for, and why

  • bookmarks: to read and file them
  • activeTab: to save the page you’re on
  • favicon: to show each site’s icon
  • sidePanel: to sit beside your tabs
  • unlimitedStorage: a local index, never synced

Firefox and Zen also need site access to fetch icons; private addresses are always skipped. Every detail.
